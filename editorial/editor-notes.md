# Standing editor notes

Injected into the writer prompt daily; curated by the post-run review.
Notes age out after 14 days unless re-earned. Prune freely.

- Lead with the event (squad, selection, injury, result) and make the heading carry that news; never headline an old appointment, a pundit's verdict or a 'no regrets' quote when a fresher event sits higher in the shortlist. _(added 2026-11-02)_
- End the paragraph on a sourced fact; drop generic closers such as 'the question is whether...', 'the bigger horizon' or 'margins matter'. _(added 2026-11-02)_
- Lead with a story about THIS team (its squad, selection, result, injury, coach). A rival's injury, a club derby or a foreign player's transfer that only mentions the team does not qualify; take a team-specific shortlist item even if it is BACKGROUND. _(added 2026-11-02)_
- Every name and claim in the heading (scorers, 'debutant', durations) must appear in and be supported by your paragraph; if the body cannot back it, cut it from the heading. _(added 2026-11-02)_
- When leading from a club match or pundit column, headline what actually happened (result, scorer, or the pundit's argument). Name the club in the heading and first sentence. Do not frame it as national selection news unless the source says so. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Prefer a shortlist item with concrete facts (score, named players, decision) over one that is only a headline. Prefer the post-match result over a pre-match item on the same fixture. Never write sentences describing what the coverage lacks. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Lead with a reported event (selection, injury, result, signing) over a pundit's column or podcast opinion; if opinion is all there is, attribute it and state the news fact it rests on. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Do not restate the heading in the opening sentence or repeat a fact later in the paragraph; use the space for what was actually said or what happened. _(added 2026-11-01)_
