# Standing editor notes

Injected into the writer prompt daily; curated by the post-run review.
Notes age out after 14 days unless re-earned. Prune freely.

- When leading from a club match or pundit column, headline what actually happened (result, scorer, or the pundit's argument). Name the club in the heading and first sentence. Do not frame it as national selection news unless the source says so. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Prefer a shortlist item with concrete facts (score, named players, decision) over one that is only a headline. Prefer the post-match result over a pre-match item on the same fixture. Never write sentences describing what the coverage lacks. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Lead with a reported event (selection, injury, result, signing) over a pundit's column or podcast opinion; if opinion is all there is, attribute it and state the news fact it rests on. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Do not restate the heading in the opening sentence or repeat a fact later in the paragraph; use the space for what was actually said or what happened. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Before saying a story is 'already reported' or 'the only new item', check the NEW/BACKGROUND flags and scores; never make that claim unless the pack shows it. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Lead with a story about THIS team: a national-team selection, injury or discipline item beats a club match report or age-grade result, and never lead with another nation's injury or news. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Lead with a story about this team's own side (squad, selection, injury, result, coach). Use an opponent's or another nation's news only when the pack has nothing about this team, and then name this team in the heading. _(added 2026-11-01)_
- Prefer reported news over pundit opinion, podcasts or old columns. If you lead with an interview or commentary piece, report what the person actually said, not background statistics or speculation. _(added 2026-11-01)_
