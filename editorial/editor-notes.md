# Standing editor notes

Injected into the writer prompt daily; curated by the post-run review.
Notes age out after 14 days unless re-earned. Prune freely.

- Never tease an unnamed detail ('a forward is undergoing an operation', 'he will get his chance'). Name the player and role, or leave the point out. _(added 2026-11-20)_
- Don't pad the paragraph with earlier-week news or a stale-dated report. Use the day's new facts and give each claim a named source. _(added 2026-11-20)_
- Rewrite every clause lifted from a source so it stands alone for a reader who hasn't seen the article; drop or explain fragments like 'once play resumed' and unexplained counts, and make sure the heading can't be read two ways. _(added 2026-11-17)_
- On first mention, give every named coach, player or pundit a one-clause role tied to this team (e.g. 'Fiji head coach Jake White', 'Ireland head coach Andy Farrell', 'former Springbok prop Steven Kitshoff'); a past job alone is not enough. _(added 2026-11-16)_
- When the body hedges a fact ('looks like', 'reportedly', 'major doubt'), keep that hedge in the heading, and check any score run or sequence against the final score and the order of events. _(added 2026-11-16)_
- When leading with a reaction, colour or human-interest story, still name the opponent and give the result or fixture in the paragraph (e.g. the scoreline or 'v Australia'), using only facts from the pack. _(added 2026-11-15)_
- Check each heading's grammar and logic: never use a venue as a team name (e.g. 'edge Genoa'), and never name one moment as decisive unless the body says it was. _(added 2026-11-15)_
- Include only facts, rumours and characterisations stated in the cited item. Never add scouting colour, 'rumoured' gossip or outlook lines such as 'adds to the attacking case'. _(added 2026-11-15)_
